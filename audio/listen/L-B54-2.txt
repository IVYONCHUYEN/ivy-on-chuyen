People often ask me what a school psychologist actually does. Most imagine that I spend all day talking to students with serious problems, but in fact that's only about a third of my job. A large part of my week is spent training teachers to recognise early signs of anxiety, such as a sudden drop in marks or a student withdrawing from friends. I also run peer support groups, where older students are trained to listen to younger ones. Students often find it easier to open up to someone close to their own age. Of course, all individual sessions are completely confidential, unless a student is in danger. The hardest part of the job is the waiting list – there are simply not enough psychologists in schools. But the most rewarding moment is when a student who arrived in tears leaves the room smiling. If you're thinking of this career, you'll need a degree in psychology and, above all, a lot of patience.
