#speakers: Host=Charon, Linh=Kore
Host: Today I'm talking to Linh, a university student who volunteered after last year's floods in central Viet Nam. Linh, why did you decide to go?
Linh: Well, my grandparents live in Quang Binh, so at first I just wanted to check they were safe. But when I saw how many families had lost everything, I couldn't just go home.
Host: What did you actually do there?
Linh: People imagine volunteers rescuing people from boats, but that was the army's job. We mostly sorted relief supplies and cooked meals in a school that was being used as a shelter.
Host: Was it difficult?
Linh: Physically, yes – we worked twelve-hour days. But honestly the hardest part was emotional. Some children had lost their homes and their schoolbooks, and they kept asking when they could go back to class.
Host: Has the experience changed you?
Linh: Definitely. I used to think climate change was something for the future. Now I know it's already affecting people. I've joined a group that teaches communities how to prepare for floods, because prevention is far cheaper than recovery.
Host: Any advice for young people who want to help?
Linh: Don't just turn up. Register with an organisation first – otherwise you can get in the way.
