Host: Dr Mai, why do so many students procrastinate?
Mai: Most people think it's simply laziness, but research shows it's usually about emotions. We put off tasks that make us feel anxious or bored. Perfectionists are especially at risk, because they're afraid the result won't be good enough.
Host: So what can students do?
Mai: The first step is to break a big task down into very small steps. 'Write an essay' feels overwhelming; 'write the first sentence' does not. Second, remove distractions – put your phone in another room. And third, reward yourself after each step.
Host: Some people say they work best under pressure, the night before a deadline.
Mai: I hear that a lot. They may finish the work, but studies show the quality is usually lower and stress levels are much higher. So I'm not convinced.
Host: Is willpower the key?
Mai: Less than you'd think. Willpower is limited. It's far more effective to build habits, so that studying becomes automatic, like brushing your teeth.
Host: What are you working on now?
Mai: I've just finished a study on teenagers and phones. Next, I'm hoping to write a book for parents.
