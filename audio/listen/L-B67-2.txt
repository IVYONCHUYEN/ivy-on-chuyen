Good afternoon. I'm a lacquer artist, and I'd like to tell you about my work. Lacquer painting has been practised in Viet Nam for centuries, but in the 1930s, artists at the Indochina Fine Arts College turned it from a decorative craft into a form of fine art. The process is extremely slow. Each painting needs between ten and twenty layers of lacquer, and after every layer the surface must be dried and then polished by hand. A large work can take me up to six months. The most difficult part is that you never know exactly how the colours will look until the final polishing – that's what makes it exciting. These days, many young people prefer digital art, which worries me. That's why I run free classes for teenagers every Sunday morning.
